\chapter{Neural Networks}
\label{c3:ch:nn}

{\large\itshape Feedforward, backpropagation and optimization, worked through by hand before a
line of the network is written.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item An introduction to neural networks: feedforward, backpropagation, gradient descent, and
    activation functions.
  \item Training neural networks by hand.
  \item A dense layer interface, and a stub implementation of it.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

This chapter moves from a single linear model to neural networks: layers of nodes, each holding a
bias and a weight per input, whose outputs are passed through an activation function and on to the
next layer. It explains what happens during feedforward and backpropagation, how the parameters are
optimized from the errors backpropagation computes, and what the activation functions are for. You
should come out of it able to explain concepts such as layers, nodes, and weights, and able to train
a small neural network by hand.

Read \secref{c3:sec:nn} first; the three hand-training exercises in \secref{c3:sec:exercises} then
put its equations to work on two small networks, and are the best way to find out whether the
theory has stuck. \Secref{c3:sec:dense} introduces the dense layer interface you start building in
the second half of the exercises, together with a stub implementation of it. That pair is the
foundation the network in Chapter~\ref{c4:ch:network} is built on, and the real layer in
Chapter~\ref{c5:ch:dense} replaces the stub without anything else having to change.
