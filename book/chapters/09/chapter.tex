\chapter{The Max Pooling Layer}
\label{c9:ch:maxpool}

{\large\itshape A layer with nothing to learn, and gradients to route.\par}

\begin{chapterbox}{In this chapter}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A concrete class \code{ml::conv_layer::MaxPool}, on the same \code{conv_layer::Interface}
    as \code{Conv}.
  \item Its \code{feedforward()}, \code{backpropagate()} and \code{optimize()}.
  \item Gradient routing in max pooling.
  \item Layers with and without trainable parameters.
  \item Replacing \code{MaxPoolStub} in \file{factory.cpp}.
\end{itemize}
\end{chapterbox}

This chapter implements a real, working max pooling layer, \code{ml::conv_layer::MaxPool}, in the
polymorphic \code{cnn_work} pipeline from Chapter~\ref{c8:ch:conv}, replacing the
\code{MaxPoolStub} it currently uses. Keep working in the same project,
\file{lectures/L08/cnn_work}. After this chapter you should have created a concrete subclass
\code{MaxPool} that inherits \code{ml::conv_layer::Interface}, and implemented its
\code{feedforward()}, \code{backpropagate()} and \code{optimize()}.

The logic is the one you worked through by hand in \secref{c6:sec:exercises} and wrote as the
struct \code{ml::MaxPoolLayer} in Chapter~\ref{c7:ch:layers}. What makes the class worth a chapter
is what it doesn't have: no trainable parameters and no activation function, and still the same
interface as \code{Conv}, printed in Exercise~\ref{c8:ex:1}. The exercises in
\secref{c9:sec:exercises} are where you write it.
